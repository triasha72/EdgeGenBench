# EdgeGenBench v0.1 Results

This document records the reproducible benchmark results for EdgeGenBench
v0.1. The release evaluates surrogate accuracy, uncertainty calibration,
feasibility classification, constrained multi-objective optimization,
physics-based validation, ONNX equivalence, model size, and edge-inference
latency.

All reported metrics were generated by the repository pipeline and its
versioned configurations. EdgeGenBench uses a synthetic aircraft-design
benchmark and is intended for research and software benchmarking rather
than aircraft certification or operational design approval.

The final release run used the following environment:

Latency results are specific to this machine and software environment.
They should be treated as comparative measurements within the same run,

## Dataset and split

The versioned benchmark configuration generated 6,000 synthetic regional
aircraft design cases.

| Partition | Rows |
|---|---:|
| Training | 4,200 |
| Validation | 900 |
| Test | 900 |
| Final training set after model selection | 5,100 |
| Total | 6,000 |

The overall physics-labeled feasible fraction was approximately 32.6%.

The feature set contains:

- passenger capacity;
- design range;
- cruise speed;
- battery specific energy;
- hydrogen storage efficiency;
- hybridization ratio;
- propulsion architecture.

The six surrogate targets are:

- estimated takeoff mass;
- mission energy;
- energy per passenger-kilometre;
- lifecycle-emissions proxy;
- operating-cost proxy;
- noise proxy.

## Surrogate-model comparison

Three classical multi-output surrogate approaches were evaluated:

1. FP32 Ridge regression;
2. Random Forest regression;
3. HistGradientBoosting regression.

### Aggregate test performance

| Model | Mean test NRMSE | Mean test R² | Model size | Batch-1 latency |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HistGradientBoosting | **0.062249** | **0.995171** | 7.216 MiB | 76.339 ms |
| Random Forest | 0.205386 | 0.953219 | 172.296 MiB | 14.652 ms |
| FP32 Ridge | 0.214590 | 0.937690 | **0.002473 MiB** | **0.142 ms** |

HistGradientBoosting produced the strongest predictive performance,
reducing mean normalized error substantially relative to both the Random
Forest and Ridge baselines.

FP32 Ridge was the smallest and fastest model. Its serialized artifact was
only 2,593 bytes and its mean batch-one latency was approximately
0.142 milliseconds.

Random Forest provided lower accuracy than HistGradientBoosting and had the
largest serialized model, but it was retained for the v0.1 uncertainty,
optimization, and ONNX pipeline because its ensemble structure supports
tree-based uncertainty estimation and the existing deployment workflow.

## Uncertainty quantification

The v0.1 uncertainty workflow used a Random Forest trained on a proper
training subset and calibrated on a held-out calibration subset.

| Partition | Rows |
|---|---:|
| Proper training | 3,360 |
| Calibration | 840 |
| Test | 900 |

Two uncertainty approaches were evaluated:

- empirical Random Forest tree-quantile intervals;
- split-conformal prediction intervals.

### Coverage summary

| Method | Nominal coverage | Mean empirical coverage across targets |
|---|---:|---:|
| Random Forest tree quantiles | 90% | 96.20% |
| Split conformal | 80% | 78.46% |
| Split conformal | 90% | 89.54% |
| Split conformal | 95% | 95.15% |

The tree-quantile intervals were conservative: target-level empirical
coverage ranged from approximately 92.56% to 98.11% for a nominal 90%
interval.

The split-conformal intervals tracked their nominal levels more closely:

| Nominal level | Lowest target coverage | Highest target coverage |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 80% | 75.56% | 80.33% |
| 90% | 87.11% | 91.22% |
| 95% | 93.22% | 96.44% |

The widest normalized intervals occurred for energy per
passenger-kilometre and noise. This indicates that relative uncertainty is
larger for targets whose absolute scales are small or whose local behavior
is less consistently captured by the Random Forest.

The conformal implementation provides marginal held-out coverage for the
synthetic benchmark. It does not establish coverage under arbitrary
distribution shift or on real aircraft data.

## Optimization-threshold selection

An initial optimization run used the classifier threshold of 0.30. Although
the classifier performed strongly on the random test set, physics-based
validation showed that the threshold was insufficiently conservative for
optimizer-selected designs.

A threshold sensitivity study was therefore performed. Each threshold used
the same:

- trained feasibility classifier;
- trained Random Forest surrogate;
- optimization configuration;
- deterministic random seed;
- 20,000 candidate designs;
- physics-based validation procedure.

The classifier itself was not retrained. Only the optimization acceptance
threshold changed.

### Broad threshold sweep

| Threshold | Accepted candidates | Accepted fraction | Pareto designs | Pareto agreement | Representative agreement |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.30 | 5,687 | 28.43% | 52 | 84.62% | 25% |
| 0.50 | 5,413 | 27.07% | 47 | 100% | 100% |
| 0.70 | 5,333 | 26.66% | 46 | 100% | 100% |
| 0.90 | 5,094 | 25.47% | 43 | 100% | 100% |

### Refined threshold sweep

| Threshold | Accepted candidates | Accepted fraction | Pareto designs | Pareto agreement | Representative agreement |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.35 | 5,562 | 27.81% | 46 | 93.48% | 25% |
| 0.40 | 5,487 | 27.43% | 44 | 100% | 75% |
| 0.45 | 5,437 | 27.18% | 50 | 98% | 100% |
| **0.50** | **5,413** | **27.07%** | **47** | **100%** | **100%** |

Threshold 0.50 was selected because it was the smallest tested value that
achieved both:

- 100% physics-feasibility agreement across the complete Pareto front;
- 100% physics-feasibility agreement across the four representative
  designs.

Higher thresholds also achieved full agreement but removed more candidates
and reduced Pareto-front diversity without improving the validation result.

EdgeGenBench therefore uses two explicitly different thresholds:

| Threshold | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 0.30 | Validation-selected classifier operating threshold |
| **0.50** | Physics-validated conservative optimization threshold |

This separation prevents the optimizer from relying on a permissive
classification boundary selected for randomly sampled test cases.

| Feasibility classifier | 5.741 MiB |
| ONNX Random Forest surrogate | 108.956 MiB |

The FP32 Ridge model is the most compact artifact by a wide margin.

The ONNX export reduced the Random Forest surrogate size from approximately
172.30 MiB to 108.96 MiB. The feasibility classifier decreased from
approximately 5.74 MiB to 2.73 MiB.

Although ONNX reduced both model sizes, the Random Forest surrogate remains
large for memory-constrained edge devices. Compact neural surrogates,
quantization, and hardware-aware model selection remain future work.

## Interpretation

The v0.1 results support the following conclusions:

1. HistGradientBoosting provides the strongest classical surrogate accuracy
   on the synthetic benchmark.

2. FP32 Ridge provides the smallest artifact and lowest classical-model
   latency, demonstrating the accuracy-versus-deployment trade-off.

3. Split-conformal intervals provide empirical coverage close to their
   requested nominal levels on the held-out synthetic test set.

4. The feasibility classifier performs strongly on randomly sampled test
   data, but its validation-selected threshold is too permissive when used
   directly inside optimization.

5. A separate optimization threshold of 0.50 is required to obtain robust
   physics-feasibility agreement for optimizer-selected designs.

6. Physics reevaluation confirms all final representative designs and all
   final Pareto designs as feasible at the selected optimization threshold.

7. ONNX conversion preserves predictions closely while substantially
   reducing CPU inference latency.

8. The Random Forest deployment remains comparatively large, motivating
   compact neural surrogates, pruning, and quantization in future releases.

## Limitations

The reported results have several important limitations:

- The benchmark dataset is synthetic.
- The physics model is an analytical research proxy rather than a
  high-fidelity aircraft simulation.
- No flight-test or certification data are used.
- The benchmark considers one versioned mission and design-space
  configuration.
- The final representative physics-validation statistics use four designs.
- Threshold selection is based on the current deterministic candidate set
  and should be reevaluated under new missions or design spaces.
- Conformal coverage is demonstrated under the current held-out split, not
  under arbitrary distribution shift.
- Latency is measured on one ARM64 macOS machine.
- No embedded GPU, NPU, microcontroller, or mobile-device benchmark is
  included.
- FP16 and INT8 quantization are not included in v0.1.
- EdgeGenBench is not a certified safety-critical engineering tool.
